What is the CISA KEV catalog?
What it is The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog: CISA’s list of CVEs with reliable evidence of exploitation in the wild.
Who maintains it The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA).
How a CVE gets added Three criteria: an assigned CVE ID, reliable evidence of active exploitation (scanning, research or a public proof of concept do not count), and a clear remediation action such as a vendor update.
Who must act on it Federal civilian executive branch agencies, which must remediate listed CVEs by each entry’s due date under Binding Operational Directive 22-01. Other organizations are not bound by it; CISA recommends they prioritize KEV entries too.
Ransomware flag Each entry carries a Known / Unknown flag for use in ransomware campaigns; the ransomware share above counts the “Known” entries.

Reading the numbers
A CVE only enters the KEV catalog once CISA confirms active exploitation in the wild, so the monthly addition rate is a floor on real-world exploitation activity, not a count of disclosed vulnerabilities.
Federal agencies must remediate each entry by its CISA due date under BOD 22-01; most organizations treat KEV listing as a patch-now signal regardless of CVSS score.
Vendor concentration reflects both install base and attacker focus. A vendor appearing here repeatedly is a prioritization signal for patch management programs.
