[SMM Weekly Review: Polysilicon Production Cuts May Fall Short of Expectations, Price Spread Between High-Efficiency and Standard Modules Continues to Widen] This week, the polysilicon price index stood at 41.09 yuan/kg, with N-type recharging polysilicon quoted at 39.5-42.6 yuan/kg and granular polysilicon at 40-41 yuan/kg. The polysilicon market weakened overall this week, as the settlement of some earlier low-price orders, combined with policy implementation falling short of expectations and continued declines in downstream prices, steadily eroded market expectations. Polysilicon: This week, the polysilicon price index stood at 41.09 yuan/kg, with N-type recharging polysilicon quoted at 39.5-42.6 yuan/kg and granular polysilicon at 40-41 yuan/kg. The polysilicon market weakened overall this week. The combination of earlier low-price order transactions, policy implementation falling short of expectations, and continued downstream price declines steadily eroded market expectations. Some manufacturers that had originally planned maintenance in December postponed it, and subsequent production cuts may also fall short of expectations, making a further polysilicon price decline more likely.

Wafer: Wafer prices extended their downward trend this week. N-type 183 wafers were priced at 0.88-0.899 yuan/piece, 210R wafers at 0.947-0.983 yuan/piece, and 210mm wafers at 1.078-1.094 yuan/piece. Some manufacturers, pressured by year-end shipment targets and cash flow constraints, proactively cut prices to move inventory, with low-priced supply disrupting the market. Top-tier players also signaled potential price adjustments. On the cost side, polysilicon remained in the doldrums with further downside expected, weakening cost support. On the demand side, downstream purchase willingness stayed subdued, with insufficient direct procurement demand. Overall, weak cost-side expectations are dragging on prices, and wafer prices are likely to consolidate with slight declines in the near term. Key factors to watch include top-tier manufacturers' pricing adjustments, polysilicon and solar cell price trends, and marginal changes in downstream procurement.

Solar cell: The solar cell market remained divergent this week. Prices for 183 and 210N held steady, while 210R continued to drift lower, and overall inventory edged up slightly. On pricing, the 183 price range held at 0.29-0.296 Yuan/W. With no market catalyst, manufacturers showed little inclination to adjust prices, and stability is expected to persist for now. The 210N price range held at 0.31-0.315 Yuan/W, supported by rigid demand in the short term. Some solar cell plants and module makers indicated that this round of market conditions is expected to last only until late November or early December. As module-side projects are gradually delivered and demand pulls back, prices may face a correction. The 210R price range was revised down to 0.296-0.305 Yuan/W. Upstream bearish sentiment persists, and manufacturers generally expect prices to converge toward 0.29 Yuan/W, but for now all parties remain in a wait-and-see mode, with no 0.29 Yuan/W low-price transactions yet. Near-term prices are expected to continue drifting lower. In November, solar cell supply remained at low levels and contracted further, with reductions coming mainly from integrated enterprises cutting module scheduled production to digest module inventory, along with matching cell production cuts. Actual output will still depend on how each manufacturer executes its production plans. Industry inventory edged up slightly this week but remained within a reasonable range. Slower 183 shipments and reduced 210R exports were the main reasons for the inventory rebound, while 210N shipments were normal and inventory remained relatively stable. The rebound in inventory has weakened price support, and subsequent focus should be on downstream procurement pace and marginal changes in export orders.

China modules: As of now, the average price of China Topcon module-182mm (distributed) is 0.6975 yuan/W; Topcon module-210mm (distributed) is 0.7175 yuan/W; Topcon module-210R (distributed) is 0.714 yuan/W; Topcon module-210R high-power (distributed) is 0.756 yuan/W; BC module-210R (distributed) is 0.7625 yuan/W; HJT module-210mm (distributed) is 0.7205 yuan/W. On the distributed side, 182mm edged down 0.002 yuan/W, HJT edged down 0.0005 yuan/W, while 210R high-power rose another 0.001 yuan/W to 0.756 yuan/W, marking a second consecutive week of countertrend rise, with the price spread between high-efficiency and standard products continuing to widen. On the centralized side, 182 module prices were 0.689 yuan/W, 210 module prices were 0.700 yuan/W, and 210R module prices were 0.695 yuan/W. Recently, solar cell 210R continued to decline and approached the key level of 0.29 yuan/W, and module cost support edged down slightly, but modules remain in a loss-making state, so the willingness to cut prices further is relatively limited. Currently, only some module enterprises pushing for annual targets at year-end have considered lowering quotes to take orders, while most top-tier producers still prefer to keep prices largely stable. On the inventory side, China module finished-product inventory at producers rebounded slightly, edging up WoW, bringing an end to the previous four consecutive weeks of destocking, consistent with the pace of lower production schedules and the approaching delivery off-season. Inventory is expected to move sideways in the 24-25GW range in the short term.

Module FOB exports:

This week, module FOB export prices were largely stable overall. For standard TOPCon modules, the upper end of quotes from some producers edged up slightly; high-power TOPCon quotes were basically stable; BC module quotes from a few producers were lowered, with overall price range changes limited. Mainstream producers showed low willingness to make further concessions. Some enterprises, affected by euro depreciation and exchange rate pressure, have expectations of raising prices, but a broad increase has not yet materialized. By market, European demand has not improved significantly yet, with clients pushing for lower prices while producers intend to hold prices steady. Some producers are considering adopting fixed reference exchange rates for quotes to cope with exchange rate fluctuations, and willingness to lower euro quotes is limited in the short term. In Southeast Asia, some quotes and client-acceptable target prices edged up slightly, but actual transactions still need to follow through. Approaching year-end, some enterprises are still mainly executing earlier orders and rushing to complete deliveries before the winter holiday. FOB prices are expected to remain largely stable in the short term, with some localized quotes likely to edge up, while actual outcomes still depend on order intake and market acceptance. As of now, FOB prices for PV module exports are as follows: TOPCon 182mm modules at $0.103–0.109/W, 210mm and 210R modules both at $0.104–0.110/W; high-power TOPCon modules at $0.109–0.114/W; BC 210R modules at $0.110–0.119/W, and BC 54-cell modules at $0.129–0.140/W.

Module CIF Southeast Asia:

This week, CIF prices for modules across Southeast Asian markets remained stable, with conventional module quotes largely unchanged in major markets and limited transactions. The market continued to operate at low levels, with prices lacking drivers for adjustment. In terms of specification structure, high-end quotes for 210mm modules in Malaysia remained lower than those for 210R, and the pattern of price concessions to compete for orders on conventional specifications in the centralized market remained unchanged. As of today, CIF prices for TOPCon modules in Southeast Asian markets are as follows:

In Malaysia, CIF prices for 182mm modules were $0.109–0.116/W, 210mm modules at $0.110–0.117/W, and 210R modules at $0.110–0.118/W;

In Indonesia, CIF prices for distributed TOPCon modules were $0.110–0.115/W;

In Thailand, CIF prices for 182mm modules were $0.107–0.115/W, 210mm modules at $0.109–0.116/W, and 210R modules at $0.108–0.116/W;

In Vietnam, CIF prices for 182mm modules were $0.105–0.113/W, 210mm modules at $0.107–0.114/W, and 210R modules at $0.106–0.114/W.

By market, an official from the Directorate General of New Energy and Renewable Energy at Indonesia's Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources stated in Jakarta on November 18 that the special presidential regulation for the 100 GWp PV plan has completed inter-ministerial coordination and entered the finalization stage. PV-related provisions will be issued separately and will no longer be incorporated into the revision of Presidential Regulation No. 112/2022, but the official release date has not yet been specified. Before the regulation is issued, large-scale tenders are difficult to launch, providing limited boost to module procurement in the short term. In the short term, there are no new demand catalysts in the region: distributed PV policies in Thailand and Vietnam are still in the early stages of implementation, and Indonesia's large-scale plan, though in the regulation finalization stage, has not yet translated into orders. CIF prices for modules in Southeast Asia are expected to remain largely stable at low levels, with the possibility of slight price concessions for conventional specifications such as 210mm in the centralized market.

India modules: This week, prices for DCR and non-DCR modules in India remained largely stable overall. The average price for DCR modules held at around $0.22/W, while non-DCR module prices remained at low levels, with quotes concentrated near $0.13–0.14/W. As of today, India-made DCR TOPCon module prices were $0.217-0.235/W, while non-DCR TOPCon module prices were $0.132-0.142/W. For DCR modules, producers diverged: Tier-1 producers held quotes largely firm, while some Tier-2 producers still offered small concessions to close deals. Year-end local project deliveries provided some support, but some distributors paused procurement amid heavy wait-and-see sentiment. On the procurement side, large IPPs and EPC enterprises again held off large orders this week, waiting for DCR-related arrangements to become clearer before locking in volumes. On the inventory side, DCR module inventory trended downward earlier this week, and the decline widened today, with the market still tracking the subsequent destocking pace. For non-DCR modules, projects that can still use non-DCR modules accelerated procurement and delivery this week to secure arrival before the grid connection deadline, creating a phased concentration of short-term procurement demand. However, exports to the US were blocked, and supply shifted to the local market, keeping supply pressure intact and prices at low levels. In the short term, DCR prices are expected to remain largely stable, while non-DCR prices fluctuate with procurement pace and stay at low levels.

European modules:

This week, European warehouse module prices were largely stable overall, and suppliers showed a stronger willingness to raise quotes. The euro weakened recently, and based on the pegged exchange rate, euro-denominated sales revenue faced conversion pressure. Suppliers tended to adjust quotes to maintain margins, but whether price hikes can be implemented still depends on client acceptance. By product type, high-power modules above 650W in large format and above 485W in small format held relatively firm prices and maintained normal transaction levels. On the demand side, high-power products still saw some procurement demand, but supply and the corresponding market size were relatively limited, not enough to lift the overall transaction center. Most clients still prioritized low-priced sources as their main procurement choice, with low acceptance of price increases. In the short term, exchange rate pressure may push some suppliers to try raising euro quotes, and high-power product prices also have some support, but price-sensitive demand still dominates, and there is resistance to transmitting price hikes to transactions. European warehouse module prices are expected to remain largely stable, with some product quotes possibly seeing tentative increases. As of now, duty-paid ex-warehouse TOPCon module prices in Rotterdam were: distributed 210R (48-cell) modules at 0.1060-0.1147 euro/W, distributed 210R (66/72-cell) modules at 0.1009-0.1112 euro/W; centralized 210R (66/72-cell) modules at 0.0983-0.1085 euro/W, and centralized 210mm (66/72-cell) modules at 0.0985-0.1089 euro/W. Based on the midpoint prices of each format, warehouse prices in Portugal and Greece were both about 2.3%-2.4% higher than in Rotterdam.

PV glass: This week, mainstream quotations for PV glass remained stable. As of November 19, the average price of 3.2mm double-layer coating held steady at 18 yuan/m2, while the average price of 2.0mm double-layer coating stayed firm at 12 yuan/m2. Overall quotation centers showed no downward movement during the week, but the actual transaction price range widened notably, with the market entering a phase of price differentiation and consolidation. In terms of transactions, module makers showed divergent acceptance of high glass prices: enterprises with relatively ample raw material inventories displayed strong resistance, with their intended and negotiated prices relatively low, while some enterprises with depleted inventories and spot orders mostly settled in the higher range, increasing the dispersion between actual transactions and quotations. SMM analysis indicates that the overall glass quotations holding at high levels this week was partly attributable to the industry's joint efforts to hold prices firm, as no substantive effective reduction occurred on the supply side. On the demand side, module enterprises mainly focused on drawing down their own inventories, with insufficient purchasing intensity for glass. Whether this round of high prices can actually be realized at settlement still hinges on two factors: first, the pace of inventory consumption on the module side, and second, whether the glass side can achieve effective supply repair. In the short term, market prices may continue the trend of differentiated consolidation, and the actual settlement center of transactions remains to be further verified.

Film: This week, film price range stood at 28,200-28,400 yuan/mt, with enterprise quotations overall stable and no notable adjustments. On the cost side, upstream resin prices have had limited disturbance on film production costs recently; downstream module demand pulled back MoM. Currently, film prices in the market are likely to remain largely stable in the short term, with the key focus ahead on the month-end settlement prices of leading petrochemical plants on the resin side. If settlement prices see significant adjustments, they will be transmitted to film pricing.

EVA: This week, spot prices of PV-grade EVA resin stood at 10,600-10,700 yuan/mt, with the market in the doldrums. New settlement price guidance from leading petrochemical plants has yet to be released, and spot quotation centers among traders edged lower. Oil prices pulled back recently, weakening cost support from raw materials for resin, while non-PV grades extended losses and were unable to provide upward momentum for PV grades. On the demand side, downstream purchase willingness was insufficient and trading sentiment was sluggish. Although supply has contraction expectations, demand weakened in tandem, leaving a limited supply-demand gap. Resin prices are expected to extend the weak pattern, with subsequent focus on raw material trends and leading petrochemical settlement prices.

POE: This week, China's delivered prices of PV-grade POE resin stood at 13,300-13,500 yuan/mt, stabilizing after an earlier restorative upward adjustment. On the demand side, downstream film plants only made just-in-time procurement. Although maintenance at some facilities and the relatively high level of current raw material prices provided some support to the bottom of POE prices, under the dual pressure of downward cost expectations and sluggish end-use demand, PV-grade POE market prices overall still lacked momentum for sustained upward breakthroughs.

High-purity quartz sand: This week, inner-layer sand prices were 40,000-46,000 yuan/mt, middle-layer sand 20,500-24,000 yuan/mt, outer-layer sand 12,000-18,000 yuan/mt, and imported spot sand 49,000-53,000 yuan/mt. Crucible prices held at 5,300-5,500 yuan/piece for 33-inch and 6,250-6,500 yuan/piece for 36-inch, with prices basically stable across the industry chain. Production cuts and suspensions at sand enterprises are materializing, but inventory has yet to show a turning point. Crucible enterprises are purchasing according to order pace and have no intention of stockpiling for the off-season, leaving neither buyers nor sellers motivated to proactively shift prices. Whether current prices can remain stable depends on inventory: if sand enterprise inventory begins to destock, quotes have conditions to hold; if off-season demand pulls back further and inventory keeps rising, localized under-the-table discounts will appear before official quote changes. Short-term judgment remains unchanged: quotes stable, transactions flexible. The downward pressure on wafer prices has not yet transmitted to the sand segment, but the transmission lag is shortening, and attention should be paid to whether crucible plants' monthly procurement volumes show a stepwise decline.

Module recycling: From November 11 to 18, China's PV module recycling market prices pulled back slightly. Average prices for early small-format framed modules fell by 0.5 yuan/piece, with first-hand reference prices for framed single-glass modules at 96-111 yuan/piece. Average prices for frameless modules fell by 25-28 yuan/mt, with single-glass quotes at 2,032-2,082 yuan/mt. Large-format module prices also pulled back, with average prices for framed modules down 0.5 yuan/piece and frameless modules down 22.5-26 yuan/mt. Over the same period, spot prices of major recycled metals weakened. SMM 1# silver (Ag99.99%) average spot price fell for consecutive days from 12,495 yuan/kg on November 11 to 11,103.5 yuan/kg on November 18. SMM A00 aluminum average spot price rose first then fell, pulling back slightly from 22,690 yuan/mt to 22,540 yuan/mt.
