Solar Glass Prices Rise as 2.0mm Average Reaches 12.5 Yuan per Square Meter

Mr. Julian Thorne

Time : Nov 18, 2026

On November 18, 2026, the Silicon Industry Branch of the China Nonferrous Metals Industry Association published its latest solar glass transaction prices, reporting an average of 12.5 yuan per square meter for 2.0mm glass. Prices for the thinner product ranged from 12 to 13 yuan per square meter, while 3.2mm glass averaged 20.5 yuan and reached a high of 21 yuan per square meter.
Prices rose further from earlier levels, a movement that points to an improvement in the balance between supply and demand. The figures offer a snapshot of the market, although the price report alone does not establish how long the increase will continue or how individual contracts will be affected.

What the reported prices show
The average price of 3.2mm solar glass was 8 yuan per square meter above that of 2.0mm glass. The two figures provide a useful reference for comparing the reported prices of the two thicknesses, but they do not by themselves explain the reasons for the difference.
For 2.0mm glass, the reported high and low were each 0.5 yuan per square meter away from the average. This range shows that transactions were recorded at different price levels; the average should therefore be read as a market indicator rather than the price of every transaction. For 3.2mm glass, the reported high was 0.5 yuan above the average.
Where the increase could be felt
Solar glass buyers and suppliers are likely to focus first on whether the higher market prices are reflected in new negotiations. For a buyer assessing glass costs, the effect will depend on the thickness purchased, the price agreed and the terms of the transaction. Existing arrangements may respond differently from new purchases.
For suppliers, higher transaction prices can affect sales revenue per square meter, but the price figures alone do not establish a change in profit. Costs, volumes and contract terms also matter. Similarly, the reported increase does not reveal how much of any additional glass cost might be passed to the next stage of the supply chain.
The further rise is consistent with a stronger supply-demand balance, but it does not identify the contribution of changes in supply or demand. Subsequent price releases can help show whether the movement persists across both reported thicknesses and whether the range of transaction prices changes. Those observations would provide a clearer basis for assessing the duration and breadth of the current increase.
