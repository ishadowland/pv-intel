Tender volume contraction resonates with weakening ex-China sentiment, module high- and low-efficiency product trends diverge [SMM Analysis]

SMM, November 26:

Since September, PV module prices have continued to decline mildly. As of late November, the average distributed price of TOPCon 210R has pulled back slightly from early September, while the centralized price has fallen more deeply, with the low end loosening more noticeably. In sharp contrast to conventional products, high-efficiency TOPCon and BC module prices have shown strong resilience, and the centralized BC price has even edged up against the trend, significantly deepening the price divergence across technology routes. On the demand side, the pace of tenders for centralized projects in China has slowed markedly, and the ex-China market has been affected by trade policy uncertainty and year-end restocking pace, with export sentiment turning more cautious. On the supply side, module production has remained at a high level for the year, and inventories have continued to destock, but their marginal support for prices has weakened. Looking ahead to December, against a backdrop of limited demand growth and ample supply, conventional module prices are expected to continue consolidating at lows, with distributed quotes relatively firm and the low end of centralized prices likely to edge down further, while the premium for high-efficiency products is expected to hold.

1. Prices: Grinding lower continues, with the low end of centralized prices loosening more visibly

Recent SMM average prices for conventional TOPCon 210R modules show that the distributed price has gradually declined from 0.720 yuan/W in early September to 0.714 yuan/W in late November. Over the same period, the centralized price fell from 0.706 yuan/W to 0.695 yuan/W, and its low end slid from 0.690 yuan/W in early September all the way down to 0.677 yuan/W, a loosening magnitude clearly larger than the average price suggests.

It is worth noting that this round of decline has not been a one-way sharp drop, but rather a rhythm of "slow decline—plateau—another slow decline": from mid-October to early November, distributed quotes consolidated near 0.715 yuan/W for nearly a month, but edged down again after mid-November, and the low end of centralized prices also stepped down to a new level in late November. The spread between distributed and centralized prices widened slightly from about 0.014 yuan/W in early September to 0.019 yuan/W, reflecting that centralized projects are more price-sensitive and price-cut pressure transmits more directly, while distributed channel prices are relatively stickier.

In sharp contrast to the grinding lower of conventional products, high-efficiency TOPCon and BC module prices have shown clear resilience. The SMM-assessed average distributed price of high-power (high-efficiency) TOPCon 210R modules has only gradually pulled back from 0.760 yuan/W in early September to 0.756 yuan/W in late November, a cumulative decline of about 0.5%, clearly smaller than the conventional segment, and has shown the first signs of stabilization. For BC modules, the average distributed price of 210R is basically flat at 0.763 yuan/W, with a full-quarter fluctuation range of less than 0.002 yuan/W. The average centralized price of BC 210R has risen against the trend from 0.749 yuan/W in early September to 0.759 yuan/W in late November, a cumulative increase of about 1.3%. Against the backdrop of conventional TOPCon centralized prices falling about 1.6% over the same period, the two have diverged by nearly 3 percentage points, making this the most prominent structural divergence in the current module market.

The firmness of high-efficiency product prices is mainly supported by structural differences on both the supply and demand sides. On the supply side, high-efficiency and BC capacity remains relatively scarce, with high concentration among top-tier players who generally produce based on sales, resulting in less shipment pressure compared with conventional modules. On the demand side, end-users have relatively high acceptance of the per-unit cost reduction from high-power products, and some centralized project tenders list high-efficiency products separately, giving them an independent bargaining position from conventional product competition. In an environment of overall oversupply and persistently falling conventional quotes, high-efficiency products have not only held their premium but have even strengthened against the trend in some segments, and the price stratification across technology routes is deepening.

2. Demand side: Shrinking tenders and weakening ex-China sentiment create two-way pressure

The demand side is the core driver of this round of grinding lower, and the domestic and ex-China threads have resonated in Q4.

In China, the pace of centralized project tender advancement has slowed markedly recently, with newly launched ground-mounted power station tenders significantly fewer than in Q3, and the density of module centralised procurement bid openings and awards has declined simultaneously. The direct impact of shrinking tenders is that the pool of orders module enterprises can bid for has narrowed. To compete for limited orders, quote competition has intensified, and low-price centralized transactions have become frequent, becoming the main force dragging down the overall price center. With year-end approaching, some project owners have postponed tender decisions due to yield calculations and grid connection pace considerations, and signals of a near-term tender recovery are not yet evident.

Ex-China, export sentiment has turned cautious. Module exports weakened notably in Q3, with some ex-China markets affected by trade policy uncertainty, high project financing costs, and front-loaded inventories, leading to a slower procurement pace and weaker inquiries. Entering Q4, although export YoY readings have recovered somewhat, MoM momentum is insufficient, and ex-China clients generally hold a wait-and-see sentiment, with a stronger tendency to push for lower prices and delay orders. The European market is approaching its year-end installation off-season, while emerging markets are affected by exchange rate and policy disruptions. The overall pattern of "volume growth with stable prices" in exports is facing challenges, and foreign trade orders are unlikely to provide an effective boost to domestic prices.

3. Inventories: Destocking continues, but marginal support for prices is weakening

From the inventory side, module finished product inventories have continued to fall from their mid-year highs, with the destocking process extending into Q4. By late November, inventory levels had dropped to relatively low levels for the year. By stage, there was a notable inventory restock in mid-October, followed by a return to the destocking channel, and the pace of destocking has slowed somewhat since November.

It should be noted that the marginal support of destocking for prices is weakening. The current destocking stems more from enterprises' proactive adjustment of shipment pace and produce based on sales, rather than downstream demand absorbing supply. Against a backdrop of shrinking tenders and cautious export sentiment on the demand side, falling inventories are difficult to translate into effective price increases, and price elasticity is being suppressed by weak demand.

4. Outlook: December likely to consolidate at lows, with attention on year-end orders and production cuts to hold prices firm

SMM expects module prices to continue consolidating at lows in December: the distributed price is likely to consolidate on a weak note in the range of 0.710–0.716 yuan/W (including freight), while the centralized price is likely to operate in the range of 0.690–0.71 yuan/W (including freight), with the low end still having some room to edge down. The distributed-centralized price spread is expected to remain at 0.015–0.020 yuan/W. High-efficiency TOPCon and BC modules are expected to continue their independent trend, with price resilience likely to hold: against a backdrop of relatively limited BC capacity supply, centralized quotes still have some upside, and the premium between high-efficiency and conventional products may widen further.

Supporting and risk factors are as follows:

First, there is unlikely to be demand-side growth. The shrinkage in domestic tenders is difficult to reverse in the short term. Existing projects rushing for year-end grid connection provide some support to orders, but cannot reverse the loose supply-demand balance. Ex-China, cautious year-end procurement sentiment continues, and exports have limited pull on prices.

Second, there is the possibility of production cuts to hold prices firm on the supply side. If prices continue to grind lower and erode cash flow, it cannot be ruled out that top-tier players may jointly adjust production schedules and contract low-price supply, which could slow the pace of price declines. However, production cuts take time to implement, and the probability of a substantive reversal within December is low.

Overall, module prices in December are most likely to continue the "slow decline—consolidate at lows" rhythm. SMM will continue to track module scheduled production, inventories, and tender developments, and update price forecasts in a timely manner.
